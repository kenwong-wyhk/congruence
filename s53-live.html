5.3 | Hidden parts

$AE$ and $BD$ meet at $C$, $AB // DE$ and $AC = CE$. Which three facts make a condition?

Angle at $A$, side $AC$, angle at $C$: the side is between the two angles, so it is ASA.

5.3 | Quick check

C: II and III only.

ASA: $\angle BAC = \angle DEC$ (alt. ∠s), $AC = EC$ (given), $\angle ACB = \angle ECD$ (vert. opp. ∠s).

AAS: $\angle ABC = \angle EDC$ (alt. ∠s), $\angle ACB = \angle ECD$ (vert. opp. ∠s), $AC = EC$ (given).

SAS would need $BC = DC$, which nothing tells us.

5.3 | Example 3: solution (a)

After proving two triangles congruent, you can prove more.

Equal parts

The other sides and angles match.

corr. sides, ≅ △s  ·  corr. ∠s, ≅ △s

Parallel lines

Equal corresponding or alternate angles prove two lines parallel.

corr. ∠s equal  ·  alt. ∠s equal

To prove lines parallel, the reason has no "//" in it. "corr. ∠s, BF // CD" would use what you are trying to prove.

1

Hunt for the hidden pair.

Common side or ∠, vert. opp. ∠s, an isosceles triangle, parallel lines, a straight line, equal pieces added on.

2

Use only what is given or proved.

Never use a result of the congruence to prove the congruence.

3

After the congruence, prove more.

corr. sides, ≅ △s · corr. ∠s, ≅ △s · corr. ∠s equal / alt. ∠s equal for parallel lines.
