F2 Ch5 Congruence

5.4 | Does it fit?

Two polygons are congruent if

each pair of corresponding sides are equal: $AB = PQ$, $BC = QR$, $CD = RS$, $DA = SP$, and

each pair of corresponding angles are equal: $\angle DAB = \angle SPQ$, $\angle ABC = \angle PQR$, $\angle BCD = \angle QRS$, $\angle CDA = \angle RSP$.

As with triangles, the order of the letters tells you which parts correspond. If any one pair is not equal, the figures are not congruent.

5.4 | Same angles?

Only $\angle DAB$ and $\angle YZW$ are $105^\circ$, so they would have to match.

Next to $\angle DAB$: $70^\circ$ and $135^\circ$.

Next to $\angle YZW$: $50^\circ$ and $135^\circ$. No $70^\circ$.

So they are not congruent.

Same angles is not enough: they must come in the same order around the shape.

F2 Ch5 Congruence

5.4 | Always congruent?

C: II and III only.

A circle's circumference fixes its radius; a square's perimeter fixes its side. But a 2 × 6 and a 3 × 4 rectangle are both parallelograms with area 12.

Always congruent if two circles have equal radii (or areas, or circumferences); two squares have equal sides (or areas, or perimeters); two regular polygons with the same number of sides have equal sides.

5.4 | Always congruent: answer

1
